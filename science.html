How protection actually works

Four Things That Decide Whether a Guard Works

A mouthguard is a piece of engineering. These are the variables that matter — and the ones a custom build lets you control.

01 — Material Thickness

Impact energy is absorbed by material sitting between the hit and the tooth. A boil-and-bite thins out exactly where you bite hardest — the front teeth. A custom guard's thickness is set digitally before it's ever made, so the material stays where it's needed.

02 — Retention

A guard that only stays in because you're clenching is a guard that moves on impact — and one that comes out at the worst moment. Fit derived from a scan grips the dental anatomy itself, so it holds position without any jaw tension.

03 — Compliance

The single most important factor, and the one most often ignored. Athletes consistently report bulk, gagging and breathing difficulty as their reasons for leaving a guard out. A slim, comfortable guard gets worn — and a guard that gets worn is the only one that protects.

04 — Jaw Position

A guard sits between your arches, which means it changes how your jaw closes. Done badly, that's muscle strain. Done deliberately, a balanced bite reduces clenching and the tension that travels up through the jaw and neck.

An honest note

On Concussion Claims

You'll see mouthguards marketed as concussion prevention. We'd rather be straight with you about where the evidence actually stands.

Some studies have reported lower rates of concussion among athletes wearing custom-fitted mouthguards compared with stock or boil-and-bite guards. Other studies have found no significant difference. The research is genuinely mixed, and no mouthguard — ours included — should be sold as concussion-proof.

What is well established is this: a properly fitted mouthguard substantially reduces the risk of dental and soft-tissue injury to the mouth, lips and jaw. That's the protection we build for, and it's protection worth having on its own.

Professional consensus

Custom-Fit Guards Are Recommended By

Major dental and sports-medicine organizations recommend properly fitted mouthguards for athletes in contact and collision sports.

American Dental Association

Recommends mouthguards for a wide list of sports and identifies custom-fabricated guards as providing the best fit, comfort and retention.

Academy for Sports Dentistry

Advocates for properly fitted mouthguards as standard protective equipment, supplied and maintained through a dental professional.

American Association of Orthodontists

Recommends mouthguard use for athletes, including those in orthodontic treatment, where protection needs are higher still.

American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry

Supports mouthguard use for children and adolescents participating in organized sport and recreational activity.
